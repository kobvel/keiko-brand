00Keiko Coach · brand positioning · Oct 2026

A coach that
knows you.

The brand idea, the archetype, the perceptual map, the voice, the visual system and what to fix — built from 4 research notes, 18 competitors, Keiko's own prompt rules, and the moment it clicks for a real athlete.

Labels on each slide: verified read from the repo or competitor copy · judgement researcher placement, to validate with athletes · inferred from indirect evidence

01The aha moment founder account

Two moments where it clicks.

1 · Send it anything, it fits it into your plan

A friend sends you his session. You screenshot it to Keiko: "Dude, can I do this? Does it fit my plan?"

It already knows you have a race Saturday. It knows your week, your goal, your injury. It restructures the plan around his session with the right adjustments — and you know exactly what to do and when.

2 · It backs the smart call, not the big number

Planned 18 km. You felt it and stopped at 14. Keiko: good call slowing down — the week's volume was already too high.

Every other AI would have praised the kilometres. This one praised the judgement, because it knew the load. You felt it was right; the coach confirmed it. That's trust.

  • Conversational, not a block. Sessions, screenshots, how you feel — the plan moves with you.
  • It knows you. Race, goal, constraints, load, history — remembered, not re-asked.
  • You always know what to do. A decision, not a dashboard.
  • It's honest about effort. Praise goes to the right decision, not the bigger number.

This is what competitors' "adaptive" doesn't mean. Theirs is a template recalculating after a missed run. Keiko adapts in conversation, with context — and tells you the truth about it.

02Who it's for research + external read

The optimiser. Many of them do HYROX.

Who they are

Self-coached, intermediate to advanced. Data-literate, "age-grouper", "data nerd". Already runs Intervals.icu or TrainingPeaks, a Garmin or COROS, and a spreadsheet. Wants a scoreboard — which is why so many picked up HYROX.

What they want

Clarity they can trust. Too much data, not enough decision. A coach who understands the load, remembers the constraints, and says what to do — with the reasoning visible so they can check it.

What they hate

Being flattered. Being handed a template. "Crushing it" pep talk. Numbers that are confidently wrong. The Nike / hero track is a different customer — leave it.

Runners first, HYROX as the wedge. HYROX alone is too small to be the market (~600–700k race entries a season, approx.), but it is the densest optimiser cluster, nobody coaches it with AI, and its athletes are runners — "HYROX is mostly a running event". Descriptor order stays running & HYROX, never the reverse.

04Positioning statement judgement
For self-coached runners, and the HYROX athletes among them, Keiko is the coach you talk to. Send it anything — a friend's session, a new race, a sore calf — and it fits it into your plan, remembering your goal, your constraints and your history, so you always know what to do and when. Unlike plan apps that recalculate a template and chat assistants that forget you, it works from your real training record, never invents a number, never flatters you, and shows the reasoning behind every change.

Onlyness test: swap in Runna, Athletica, Strava or ChatGPT — each has a documented review that contradicts this sentence. We still avoid the word "adaptive" (8 competitors claim it) and say what it actually is: a conversation with a coach who remembers.

05Personality

70% Sage, 30% Caregiver — spoken like a coach who texts.

SAGE 70
CAREGIVER 30

Sage

Analytical, objective, truth-seeking. This is the job athletes hire a coach for: "be my brain", fix the tendencies they can't see themselves.

Caregiver

Protection from the athlete's own tendencies, no guilt mechanics. Stops the Sage reading cold. Already in the prompts: "concise, warm, and direct"; a 12-word cap on praise.

Everyman — register only

Not a third archetype. It's how the Sage speaks: plain words, no jargon, never mentions "the software behind the coach".

Guardrail: Tracksmith's "quiet expert" slid into elitism (~1,500 angry comments). Be expert about the athlete's data, never about the athlete's worth.  ·  The 70/30 was checked against an outside brand strategist's read of the audience ("optimiser, techy, finding clarity through trusted sources — less Nike/hero"): Sage stays the lead.

The honest caveat: an empty quadrant isn't automatically valuable. One Austrian pilot found athletes trusted a polished AI plan more than a human one. Keiko's hedge is that its honesty is structural — fail-closed saves, confidence lines — so it costs nothing extra if "honesty" doesn't sell as a word.

10Voice · 1 of 5 rules verified

Direct, not curt.

Good · real eval output
Updated your schedule for today to prioritize recovery after yesterday's 15.1 km long run. — Sunday, Sep 27: Full rest. Take the day completely off — no make-up work needed.
Bad
Great question! Let's take a look at where you are this week. You asked whether you should still do Sunday's session, and there are a few things to consider here. First, yesterday you ran 15.1 km, which is a significant effort…

Rule in coachResponseContract.ts: "Answer the current ask in the first sentence. Do not repeat the question."  ·  Complaint it answers: "300–600 word responses for yes/no questions".

11Voice · 2 of 5

Honest, not harsh.

Good
A 1:20 at Hamburg is not on the table from this block. Your last two sims put compromised-running pace at 4:38/km and the 1:20 line needs roughly 4:10. Twelve weeks closes some of that gap, not all of it. 1:28–1:31 is the honest range; here is how I got there.
Bad
Love the ambition! A sub-1:20 is absolutely within reach if we stay consistent and trust the process. Let's crush it together!

Rule: "Be direct. No filler, no cheerleading. Push back when the logs warrant it."  ·  Complaint: an athlete had to type "but be realistic does it make sense?"

12Voice · 3 of 5

Precise, not a dashboard.

Good
Target for the 3 × 2 km: 4:05/km. That is your 10 km race pace from Sep 14 (41:12 → 4:07/km) minus 2 s/km for the shorter reps. Hold the first rep at 4:08; it is easier to speed up than to rescue the third.
Bad
Your aerobic efficiency index rose 3.2% while cardiac drift fell to 4.1% and training stress balance sits at −18 with an acute:chronic ratio of 1.27, suggesting strong adaptation. Your VO2max estimate is 54.

Rule: "Show your arithmetic for every time/pace/gap calculation."  ·  Complaint: a 1:28 PB recalled as 1:33 and cascaded into every pace; "false precision" on station estimates.

13Voice · 4 of 5

Warm, not a cheerleader.

Good · real fixture
That was a well-controlled threshold session. You delivered the intended aerobic-pressure stimulus without turning it into a race. Keep the next easy session genuinely easy.
Bad
Amazing work today, superstar! You absolutely smashed that threshold session and should be so proud of yourself. Every step is progress. Keep shining!

Rule: acknowledgement ≤ 12 words; "Reserve praise for genuinely noteworthy decisions"; "Faster or harder than plan is a deviation, not automatically a success."  ·  Complaint: "Great question!", "Excellent decision!", "That shows real self-awareness!"  ·  Lived example: the athlete cut an 18 km run to 14 km and the coach backed the decision because the week's volume was already too high — praise for judgement, not for kilometres.

14Voice · 5 of 5

Attentive, not needy.

Good · real eval output
Adjusted today's Sunday workout to match your 60-minute cap and follow yesterday's 15.1 km run. Easy run: 50 min continuous (~9 km), HR below 150 bpm. Keep the effort fully aerobic and capped at 50 minutes.
Bad
Here's a 70-minute progression run for Sunday! Let me know how it goes, and if you want, I can also put together a nutrition plan for the week, or we could look at your shoe rotation. Would you like me to do that?

Rule: "End when the coaching is delivered. Do NOT close with engagement questions."  ·  Complaint: "If you want, I can…" endings in 90% of replies; forgot "can't run Saturday" in the same session.

17The owned motif

The reasoning block is the brand idea, rendered.

TUESDAY · KEY SESSION
3 × 2 km @ 4:05/km · 90 s jog
Why this session
10 km race pace from Sep 14 (41:12 → 4:07/km) − 2 s/km for shorter reps. Hold rep 1 at 4:08.
Review basis: 2 sims, 1 race · Confidence: high
READING THEME · WEEKLY REVIEW
Week 42 · 4 of 5 sessions as planned
The one fix
Thursday's easy run drifted to 4:55/km — 20 s faster than cap. Protect the easy days before the sim block.
Review basis: Strava + your notes · Confidence: medium
  • Small brands win with one UI motif competitors end up copying (Linear's status circles). Ours: the "why" line + review basis + confidence, on every surface.
  • Warm neutrals separate Keiko from WHOOP's #0B0B0B and Strava's black.
  • Accent is for progress and state — never fills, never gradients.
  • Sora stays. Weight contrast and lowercase labels; uppercase tracking is WHOOP's signature.
  • No orange near the mark — it reads as a Strava satellite.
  • Drop the coach-face avatar in chat; mascots are the Magician tell. Bare swirl only.
  • Imagery: ordinary adults mid-week. A Tuesday easy run, a sled in a quiet gym. Never race-day elites.
